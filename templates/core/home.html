ABOUT YALA NATIONAL PARK & THE SRI LANKAN LEOPARD

Yala National Park (Ruhuna) is internationally famous for having the highest concentration of wild leopards per square kilometer anywhere on Earth (Panthera pardus kotiya).

Because leopards in Yala are the island's supreme apex predator with no lions or hyenas to compete against, they exhibit bold diurnal hunting behavior—often seen resting atop palu trees or lounging peacefully on ancient granite rock outcrops during early morning and late afternoon game drives.

Spanning 979 square kilometers across dry zone monsoon forests, brackish lagoons, and coastal dunes, Yala encompasses five main blocks alongside Kumana and Lunugamvehera nature reserves, offering sanctuary to Asian Elephants, Sloth Bears, Mugger Crocodiles, and 215 bird species.

YALA NATIONAL PARK MAP & PARK BLOCKS GUIDE

Spanning 979 sq km along Sri Lanka's southeastern coast, Yala National Park comprises 5 distinct administrative blocks, 4 major entrance gates, and adjacent wildlife sanctuaries.

ENTRANCE GATES & ACCESS GUIDE

NAVIGATING YALA'S 4 MAIN PARK GATES

Access to Yala National Park is regulated through designated Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC) entrance gates. Selecting the right gate determines which blocks and habitats your safari jeep will explore:

Palatupana Main Gate

Tissamaharama Entry

Primary entrance to Block 1 (Ruhuna). Nearest gate for lodges in Tissa and Kirinda.

Katagamuwa Gate

Kataragama Entry

Western access gate serving both Block 1 and Block 5 (Weheragala Reservoir).

Galge Gate

Buttala Road Entry

Northern entry point dedicated to Block 3 and Block 5 for low-density safaris.

Okanda Gate

Kumana Entry

Eastern entrance to Kumana National Park (Yala East) waterbird sanctuary.

MAIN GAME DRIVE ZONE

BLOCK 1 (RUHUNA) — 141 SQ KM

World's Highest Leopard Sighting Density

Block 1 is the most celebrated sector of Yala National Park. Bordered by the Indian Ocean to the south and the Manik River to the north, it encompasses dry monsoon forests, open scrublands, coastal lagoons (Buttuwa & Uraniya), and ancient granite monoliths like Patanangala.

Key Wildlife: Sri Lankan Leopard, Asian Elephant herds, Sloth Bear, Spotted Deer, Wild Boar, Mugger Crocodiles, and Lesser Adjutant Storks.

QUIET WILDERNESS ZONE

BLOCK 5 (WEHERAGALA) — 66 SQ KM

Reservoir Ecosystem & Birding

Located near Katagamuwa and Galge gates, Block 5 surrounds the Weheragala Reservoir on the Manik River. It offers serene, low-vehicle game drives through dense riverine forest canopy and calm waters where elephants bathe during midday hours.

Key Wildlife: Solitary Bull Elephants, Sloth Bears, Crested Serpent Eagles, Brown Fish Owls, Barking Deer, and Leopard corridors.

DEEP NATURE RESERVES

BLOCKS 2, 3 & 4 — 770 SQ KM

Untamed Jungle & Ancient Ruins

Spanning vast untouched tracts from the Kumbukkan Oya river mouth to the northern boundary, Blocks 2, 3, and 4 are strict natural reserves. They shelter 2,200-year-old Buddhist monastic ruins at Sithulpawwa and Magul Maha Viharaya.

Key Wildlife: Wild Water Buffalo, Migratory Elephant Corridors, Black-necked Storks, Fishing Cats, and Sambar Deer.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

YALA NATIONAL PARK SAFARIS FAQ

Everything you need to know about planning, booking, and enjoying private 4x4 game drives and luxury camping stays in Yala.

What is the best time of day for a Yala National Park leopard safari?

Early morning game drives (6:00 AM – 10:00 AM) and late afternoon game drives (2:30 PM – 6:00 PM) offer peak wildlife activity. Leopards are boldest at dawn and dusk, actively patrolling territory or resting atop granite rock outcrops.

Which entrance gate is best for Yala Block 1 game drives?

Palatupana Gate (near Tissamaharama) is the main entrance for Block 1 (Ruhuna), which holds the world's highest leopard density per square kilometer. Katagamuwa Gate serves guests staying near Kataragama or accessing Block 1 & 5.

How much does a Yala National Park entrance permit cost?

Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC) gate tariffs are $25.00 USD for foreign adults, $15.00 USD for foreign children (ages 6–12), $20.00 USD for SAARC adults, plus LKR 300 vehicle entry licensing fee, vehicle service charge, and 18% government VAT.

Are Yala Leopard Tracks safari jeeps private or shared?

All our safari jeep packages are 100% private. You get a dedicated 4x4 safari jeep with elevated cushioned safari seating, experienced driver-trackers, chilled bottled water/refreshments, and park clearance support.

What animals can I spot in Yala National Park besides leopards?

In addition to the Sri Lankan Leopard (Panthera pardus kotiya), Yala shelters Asian Elephants, Sloth Bears, Mugger Crocodiles, Spotted Deer, Wild Boar, Sambars, Water Buffaloes, Fishing Cats, and over 215 resident and migratory bird species.
